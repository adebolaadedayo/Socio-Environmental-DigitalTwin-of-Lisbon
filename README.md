# Prioritising Nature-Based Solutions for Urban Heat Adaptation through a Socio-Environmental Digital Twin Demonstrator in Lisbon.

# Research Proposal Workflow (Thesis Work Ongoing)

## Context 

The reduction in Lisbon’s green fraction has driven significant nocturnal thermal retention, raising health 
concerns for the public during summer heatwaves  (Silva et al., 2022). As reported by Regal (2026) and TPN 
(2026), the Portuguese Institute of the Sea and the Atmosphere (IPMA) placed Lisbon under a red alert warning 
on July 2nd, 2026, due to intensified heat, with highs of around 40°C and lows between 24°C and 28°C. In 
Lisbon, factors such as the growing ageing profile, alongside spatial variations in household income and 
building quality, greatly influence the true severity of environmental hazards (Cunha et al., 2025). 

## Research gap and objectives

Existing microclimate studies focus mainly on the cooling performance of Nature-Based Solutions (NBS) in 
urban spaces without considering socio-environmental equity (Borah et al., 2026; Burkart et al., 2016; Leal 
Filho et al., 2026). Hence, this study seeks to answer the question: “How can a socio-environmental Digital 
Twin demonstrator integrating spatial heat-vulnerability mapping and 3D LiDAR-derived microclimate 
modelling effectively prioritise and quantify the human thermal comfort benefits of NBS in Lisbon's most 
vulnerable neighbourhoods?” To achieve this, the following objectives will be addressed:  
- To develop a Socio-Environmental Vulnerability Index (SEVI) using Lisbon’s census and climate data to 
identify neighbourhoods most at risk from extreme heat. 
- To combine airborne LiDAR and orthophotos to generate precise 3D canopy models and 2D ground cover 
for microclimate modelling. 
- To quantify the human thermal comfort benefits (𝑇𝑚𝑟𝑡 and PET) of NBS solutions such as street trees, 
bioretention cells and green roofs in socio-economically vulnerable neighbourhoods. 
- To develop an interactive 3D Digital Twin demonstrator to visualise the cooling benefits of NBS for 
stakeholder decision support.

## Methodological approach

This study will be executed by four main methodological approaches including: a spatial multicriteria decision 
analysis to develop a SEVI for neighbourhood prioritisation from Lisbon UrbClim and census data, the fusion of 
airborne LiDAR and orthophotos in ArcGIS Pro to produce high-resolution 3D surface and canopy models, a 
microclimate simulation using the UMEP-SOLWEIG model in QGIS, forced by ERA5 meteorological data, to 
quantify pedestrian thermal comfort using targeted NBS interventions, and the deployment of these spatial 
outputs into an interactive 3D web digital twin demonstrator using CesiumJS. 

## Expected results and contributions

This study will quantify pedestrian-level heat stress reductions for targeted NBS within highly vulnerable 
neighbourhoods in Lisbon and contribute an interactive 3D digital twin demonstrator that supports urban 
planners and non-technical decision-makers in equitable climate adaptation planning.
